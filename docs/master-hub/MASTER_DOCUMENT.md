## 3. Mission and Philosophy

### 3.1 Identity and mission

AiIA means **AI Intelligence Agency** and is the umbrella ecosystem for coordination, governance, and strategic direction across its projects. Its mission is Human Artificial Intelligence Co-Evolution: developing intelligence and capability through deliberate, structured human–AI collaboration.

> Not just creating intelligence. Learning to develop it together.

### 3.2 HAAI philosophy

Human Artificial Intelligence Co-Evolution (HAAI) is the philosophical and methodological foundation of AiIA. It emphasizes collaboration in which people retain accountability and decision authority while AI systems provide assistance, analysis, synthesis, research, and other bounded capabilities.

HAAI is a guiding philosophy, not a separate agency brand. Writing about the philosophy does not establish that it has been adopted in every process or implemented as a technical system.

### 3.3 Evidence-first development

AiIA distinguishes among:

- **Research:** exploration and information gathering; findings may remain provisional.
- **Proposal:** a suggested direction that has not necessarily been approved or implemented.
- **Specification:** a description of intended behavior or design, not proof of implementation.
- **Prototype:** an experimental artifact whose scope and limitations must be stated.
- **Verified implementation:** an implementation supported by appropriate, reproducible evidence for the specific claim being made.

A claim must not be promoted from one category to another without evidence. Human accountability and final approval remain explicit even when AI systems contribute to analysis or documentation.

## 8. Governance and Evidence Policy

### 8.1 Evidence statuses

Use these statuses consistently and explain their scope:

- **VERIFIED** — the specific claim is supported by directly inspectable, appropriate evidence.
- **PARTIALLY VERIFIED** — evidence supports only part of the claim or leaves material conditions unresolved.
- **INFERRED** — a conclusion is reasoned from available information but not directly established.
- **PROPOSED** — a suggested future action, design, or role; not necessarily approved or implemented.
- **UNKNOWN** — available evidence does not establish the claim.
- **NOT RUN** — an identified test or verification has not been executed.
- **NOT STARTED** — specified work has not begun, to the extent that this is known.
- **BLOCKED** — work cannot proceed until a stated condition is resolved.
- **REJECTED** — a proposal or claim has been explicitly rejected by appropriate authority or evidence.

### 8.2 Mandatory evidence rules

1. Unknown is not Verified.
2. A plan is not a completed action.
3. A chat report is not a GitHub commit.
4. A commit is not runtime verification.
5. A successful workflow is not proof of live deployment.
6. Every important result should have an identifiable evidence source.
7. Historical evidence must retain its date and provenance.
8. Do not blindly delete, overwrite, or move project assets.
9. Distinguish AI-generated claims from independently verified facts.
10. Consequential changes require appropriate human authorization.

Evidence should identify the exact claim supported, source artifact, date or revision where available, verification method, and any known limitations. Verification of one artifact must not be generalized to unsupported claims about the whole system.

### 8.3 Change management

Use version control to preserve history and make changes reviewable. Significant changes should be scoped, authorized, and reviewed before integration. Keep changes limited to the authorized files and objectives. Preserve prior records and provenance; do not silently replace historical information. Document decisions and status changes with their evidence basis.

## 9. Security and Risk Management

Security and auditability are design requirements, not verified properties of the current application or ecosystem. Before implementation or deployment, define and review at least:

- Access control and least-privilege permissions for people, agents, services, and repositories.
- Secret and credential handling; do not expose credentials in source, logs, or documents.
- Data classification, privacy, retention, and cross-platform sharing boundaries.
- Threat models, trust boundaries, dependency risks, and abuse or failure cases.
- Audit records, provenance, incident handling, backups, and recovery verification.
- Review and testing requirements appropriate to the risk of each change.

Record unresolved risks as UNKNOWN or BLOCKED where appropriate. A design statement or security review plan does not demonstrate that a control has been implemented or that a system is secure.
